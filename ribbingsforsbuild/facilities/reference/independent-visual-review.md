## Photo and map agreement

Club photos `club-127` and `club-136` show pale yellow vertical timber boarding, gray/gray-white trim, large lower openings, small horizontal loft windows directly below the eaves, a larger gable attic window, tile gable roof, chimney and wraparound timber deck. The facade is taller and more articulated than the current generic low single-window-row building. Exact hidden openings and metric roof/wall heights remain estimates.

The deck visible across the long facade agrees with the south/west pale deck outline in the north-up ortho. The putting green sits east of the building. Club-128 corroborates the short gable facing the putting area; the photograph's perspective should not be used as a metric footprint.

The estate aerial review shows the clubhouse and main manor as separate yellow buildings, and the lakeside boathouse as a separate small waterside landmark. The oblique aerial is useful for roof and facade interpretation; the orthophoto supplies plan coordinates. Large trees obscure some facades, small structures and ground features in both sources.

## Completeness limits

A useful initial Blender scene should include corrected clubhouse/deck, practice green, range strip/mats/low dividers/end building, major adjacent paths/parking, estate/manor roofs, farm roofs and the corrected maintenance yard. Source-only outlines can represent features awaiting photo confirmation. Photo credit/date/source links and estimate labels should remain accessible in the Blender workspace.

The newest downloaded local orthophoto is captured **2024-05-17**; 2026-09-10 is the acquisition/review date. Club architectural photographs are from **2024-07-12** according to retained EXIF metadata. These dates must not be described as a 2026 site survey. Current mat counts, furniture, temporary storage, netting, signs, internal layouts and obscured facades remain dependent on source visibility or newer confirmation.

No independently reviewed photo establishes a long driving-range side safety net. A net should not be invented merely because another course's generic range renderer supports one. Vehicles, people and loose machinery are incidental references rather than permanent surveyed facilities.
